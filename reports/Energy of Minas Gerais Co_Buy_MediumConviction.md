With CMIG4 trading on single‑digit P/E and mid‑single‑digit EV/EBITDA, our conservative DCF and peer framework indicate c.20–35% upside over 12–24 months, offering an attractive risk‑reward skew for value‑oriented investors willing to accept Brazilian regulatory and political risk.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

### 1.4 DCF-based fair value (detailed)

We build a simplified FCFE DCF using normalised FCF.

**Cost of equity**

- Brazil sovereign and regulatory risk remain high; Cemig has local AAA rating but is state‑controlled.[^2]
- Assume nominal cost of equity $r$ = 13%.

**Stage‑1 PV**

- FCF0 = 3.5bn.
- FCF1 = 3.5 × 1.03 ≈ 3.61bn; FCF5 ≈ 3.5 × 1.03^5 ≈ 4.06bn.

## 4. Sector and Macro View

### 4.1 Sector overview

- Brazil’s electric utility sector is in a mid‑cycle expansion phase, with significant investments required in distribution reliability, transmission corridors, and integration of renewable generation.[^2]
- Pricing power is largely governed by ANEEL through tariff reviews and allowed returns on assets; value creation depends on efficient capex and regulatory execution more than pure market pricing.[^2]
- The sector continues a trend of portfolio rationalisation, with disposals of minority stakes and small plants, while prioritising core regulated assets and contracted renewables.[^3][^2]
- State‑linked utilities face increasing governance and ESG scrutiny, but also benefit from strong access to capital markets, including sustainable debentures.[^2]


### 4.2 Macroeconomic sensitivities

- **Energy prices and hydrology**: PLD movements and MRE performance affect trading and generation margins; 1Q25 demonstrates sensitivity to regional price spreads and low hydrology.[^4]
- **Interest rates**: IPCA and CDI directly affect debenture costs and discount rates embedded in regulated returns; higher real rates increase WACC and may pressure valuations.[^4][^2]
- **FX (BRL/USD)**: Affects cost of imported equipment and Itaipu/legacy FX debts; Eurobond settlement reduces this but Itaipu remains.[^4][^2]
- **Domestic growth/income**: Drives electricity demand, distributed generation adoption and default/loss patterns; Cemig tracks default and losses closely and maintains ARFA>99%.[^3][^2]


### 4.3 Competitive positioning and moat

- Cemig holds a near‑monopoly in distribution in Minas Gerais and owns a significant, renewable generation portfolio (4.68GW installed, 100% renewable) plus growing solar and DG footprint.[^2]
- Extensive network, strong brand, deep local relationships and scale in free‑market trading (14% share in retail free clients) provide meaningful competitive advantages.[^2]
- Regulatory constraints are high but apply to all players; Cemig’s track record of maintaining loss and quality indicators below regulatory thresholds supports concession security.[^3][^2]

Moat assessment: **stable and gradually strengthening**, given ongoing network investments, high DG/connectivity penetration and ESG leadership.
